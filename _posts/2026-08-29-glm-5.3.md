I woke up this morning with an exciting announcement from Ollama in my mailbox: "{% include external link="https://ollama.com/library/glm-5.3" title="GLM 5.3" %} and {% include external link="https://ollama.com/library/glm-5.3-flash" title="5.3 Flash" %} models are now available".

Since I’m working on `yoker-test`, a Yoker model evaluation framework, I decided to test the current embryonic test suite with `glm-5.3-flash` to see how it would perform. To my (not so) surprise, it ran quickly and used visibly fewer credits compared to my default `glm-5.2` model.

Next, I tried to put it to work on the `yoker-test` project itself. Aside from the obvious improvement in efficiency, another important aspect stood out: this model may not be "smarter", but it does take every detail of its instructions to heart, causing it to fail on instructions where its predecessor simply carried on. This model is essentially highlighting issues in my current agent definitions and urging me to continue the migration of my agentic collective.

Next, I moved forward with implementing Ollama usage tracking in `yoker-test`’s backlog to generate its first useful metric: the total amount of Ollama credit used for the entire test suite. Here are the results from running the basic test suite on the models:

The release of GLM 5.3 clearly illustrates the pace at which these models are currently evolving: they are becoming more efficient, faster and require less and less GPU resources.

I'll be testing both new models over the coming week to see how they actually hold up.

(*) The current metrics are very rough estimates, but they adhere to the design principle I've put forward for `yoker-test`: "Tests are performed in a realistic setting to produce realistic metrics".
